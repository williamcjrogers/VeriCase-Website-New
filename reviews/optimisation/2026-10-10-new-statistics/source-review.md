# New statistics: source and publication decisions

Checked 10 October 2026. Input: the owner's pasted “Strategic Data Upgrades & Citations Report”. The whole input was read; its recommendations are research leads, not evidence of their own accuracy. The seven proposed upgrades were assessed for homepage relevance and substantiation. The appended market/profession counts were screened rather than comprehensively refreshed, since none is proposed for publication in this homepage revision.

Independent primary-source verification: stats_kcl_sources (KCL), stats_hka_giri (HKA/GIRI). Repository audit: stats_inventory. Parent reviewed FMI, BuiltWorlds and the unsupported market/workforce claims. The owner selected replacing the headline figures with 50%, 33.4% and 65.8%.

## Headline figures accepted

### 50%: respondents identifying a leading cause

[King's College London / Adjudication Society, 2024 report](https://www.kcl.ac.uk/construction-law/assets/kcl-dpsl-construction-adjudication-report-3.0-2024-update-digital-aw1.pdf#page=28), p. 28, Figure 16. Published 20 November 2024. 165 individuals answered the causes question, with multiple answers permitted; 166 answered the wider survey. It concerns statutory adjudication between 01 May 2023 and 30 April 2024. Recruitment and scope appear on p. 13; respondent backgrounds on p. 57 include quantity surveyors, solicitors and claims consultants.

Accepted meaning: 50% of respondents selected inadequate contract administration among the leading causes of adjudicated disputes. Do not convert it into 50% of disputes, a survey confined to legal practitioners, or proof that missing records caused these disputes. Contract administration is broader than record-keeping.

The [Adjudication Society](https://www.adjudication.org/) still identifies November 2024 as its latest report; [KCL](https://www.kcl.ac.uk/news/kings-publishes-third-construction-adjudication-report-focusing-on-key-trends) describes the third and final report of this project. The explicit historical dates remain important.

### 33.4% and 65.8%: HKA's investigated projects

[HKA, CRUX Insight Eighth Annual Report: From Insight to Foresight, November 2025](https://www.hka.com/news/crux-insight-eighth-annual-report-from-insight-to-foresight/), “Cumulative impacts”. The cumulative dataset covers more than 2,200 projects in 114 countries investigated by HKA consultants. Its [January 2026 explanation](https://www.hka.com/thought-leadership/whats-changing-in-global-construction-disputes/) describes the selected population as distressed projects. The [CRUX hub](https://www.hka.com/crux-insight/) continues to identify the eighth report as the latest edition.

Accepted meanings: sums in dispute averaged 33.4% of contract budgets; extensions of time sought averaged 65.8% of planned schedules. These are claims, not awards, proven entitlement, actual delays or whole-industry averages. Both numbers concern the same selected dataset. No accessible 2025 PDF was obtained, so the website cites HKA's own publication announcement without inventing a page number.

The attachment mixes report editions. The [2024 seventh report](https://www.hka.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/CRUX-Insight-2024-Changing-the-Narrative.pdf#page=4) reports 33.2% and 66.5% across 2,002 projects in 107 countries, with a June 2024 cutoff. Its Overview is printed pp. 3–4 (PDF page 4). The 67.1% time figure belongs to the older sixth-report series; it is not the companion to 33.4% in the 2025 report.

## Appendix screening

The appendix's “already verified” label is not adopted as fact. Most population, professional membership, turnover and investment figures concern investor market sizing, not the evidence problem on this page. They were not republished or fully refreshed.

Two material denominator problems were identified:

- “80% of litigation costs” is not substantiated by the named generic EDRM/ABA references. Existing project research instead records RAND's 2012 finding that review represented about 73% of the cost of producing electronic disclosure across 57 productions. That denominator is not total litigation cost. No new cost-saving claim is published.
- “London EU construction workers fell from 42% to 8%” misstates the statistic. The [CITB skills research, p. 54](https://www.citbni.org.uk/CITB/files/02/02dd7d2b-8747-4d91-96a8-7b740f23731e.pdf) concerns the share of surveyed London employers directly employing migrant workers in 2018 and 2021, not the share of the workforce who were EU nationals. It discusses both Brexit and the pandemic. The primary PDF's indexed text was available; full web-reader fetch failed. No workforce claim is published.
